what's happening

Every point starts close to the origin, packed into a small random cluster — that's the noise block you see for an instant. The very first few iterations move that whole cluster together, so it still reads as one coherent, folding sheet: your samurai sword.

Past a handful of iterations, sensitivity to initial conditions takes over — the sheet shears, folds, and self-intersects. Points that started as neighbors are flung to opposite sides of the attractor. That's the transient: states visited only finitely many times, never returned to.

Once every point has folded into the invariant set, the shape stops changing — you're watching the recurrent states, visited over and over, forever, from now on regardless of where any point began.

Coefficients a=-2, b=-2, c=-1.2, d=2 — found by rendering your final frame's silhouette against catalogued De Jong sets (Paul Bourke's archive), matching orientation and the wing/hole topology, then reproducing the fold-up from a fresh random seed and confirming it collapses into the same "sword → sheet → recurrent shell" sequence as your video.
